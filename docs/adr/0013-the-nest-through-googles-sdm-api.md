# The Nest through Google's Smart Device Management API, polled, with its own sign-in

The Hub shows the house's heating in the header, beside the outdoor weather: the inside temperature, the humidity and what the Nest is doing. The thermostat is a Nest Learning Thermostat (3rd generation). The only supported way to reach it is Google's Smart Device Management (SDM) API, through Device Access. Ross registered for Device Access on 21 November 2026, a one-time US$5 fee. A test against the Household's Nest that day (AB#42) showed it reports the room temperature, humidity, mode (only Heat and Off are available), the heat setpoint, Eco, whether it's heating, and whether it's online. Setting the heat setpoint worked too.

**The Hub's own Google project and OAuth client are used**, with a sign-in of its own. The Nest is on the same Google account as the Calendars, and the Hub's Google app is published (AB#48), so its sign-ins don't end after 7 days. The Device Access project is switched to the Hub's client. The Nest isn't added to the Household's Google sign-in, for two reasons. An SDM grant has to start at Nest's Partner Connections page (`nestservices.google.com/partnerconnections/<project ID>/auth`), where the home and the thermostat are chosen; Google's own sign-in page can't do that. And a separate refresh token, kept in `nest-token.json` beside `google-token.json` in the Hub's data folder, means the heating can be disconnected, or its sign-in can end, without touching the Calendars, the lists or the backups. Settings offers Connect the Nest and Disconnect.

**The Hub polls rather than subscribing to events.** Every 2 minutes it lists the Device Access project's devices, which reports every trait of the thermostat at once, and keeps the answer in memory. SDM can also send events through Google Cloud Pub/Sub. But that needs a Pub/Sub topic and subscription to run, and a service account whose key would live on the Pi. With one thermostat, a temperature a couple of minutes old is fresh enough for a wall screen. The polls go out from the Pi, as everything else does (ADR 0002). That's one call every 2 minutes, a tenth of the 5 a minute that Google allows a Sandbox project for listing devices, per user ([usage limits](https://developers.google.com/nest/device-access/project/limits), read 21 November 2026).

**It survives failures the way the Energy integrations do (ADR 0007).** If there's been no answer for 15 minutes, or Google says the Nest is offline for that long, the header says "Not reporting since 14:02" instead of showing an old temperature. If the Nest's sign-in has ended, it says so straight away, and Settings asks for the Nest to be connected again. A run of failures logs one line when it starts, not one every couple of minutes. The refresh token, the project ID and the device ID never appear in logs or telemetry. Logged request addresses have the project and device IDs replaced.

**The project ID is configuration, not a secret, but it's kept with them.** It goes in the Key Vault as `nest-project-id`, and the setup scripts put it on the Pi as `NEST_PROJECT_ID`, as they do the Google client ID. With a token, it's enough to reach the thermostat. Without `NEST_PROJECT_ID`, Settings doesn't offer to connect the Nest. With sample data, or with no Google client, the header shows a sample "Heating to 21°".

**What the Hub may change, from the next story (AB#46):** only the heat setpoint, between 16° and 22°, with the SDM `SetHeat` command, and only in Heat mode. Like turning the dial, the change lasts until the Nest's schedule next changes. The Hub never changes the mode, Eco or the schedule. They're shown, and changed on the Nest or in its app. This story only reads.

**The API's limits.** SDM doesn't expose the Nest's schedule, so the Hub can't say when the next change is, or what to. It knows nothing about hot water, or about Home and Away. Those stay in the Nest app.

## Considered Options

- **Add the SDM scope to the Household's Google sign-in:** one sign-in instead of two. But the thermostat has to be chosen on the Partner Connections page, and one token for everything means losing the heating loses the Calendars too, and the other way round.
- **A separate Google Cloud project and OAuth client for the Nest:** keeps the two entirely apart, but it's a second app to publish and keep in production, on the same Google account, for no gain.
- **Pub/Sub events instead of polling:** changes arrive within seconds, but it needs a topic, a subscription and a service account key on the Pi, and the API still has to be read for the first state and after any missed message.
- **The unofficial Nest API or Home Assistant's integration:** not supported by Google, and the old Works with Nest API was shut down in 2023.
